# OpenAI mathematics, constrained lineups, and Candle for football ML

Research 2026-10-11, based on the actual [OpenAI mathematics catalogue](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/main/CONTENTS.md), selected manuscript abstracts and [Lean scope for family 113](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/main/lean/docs/113.md), [Candle 0.11.0](https://github.com/huggingface/candle/blob/main/Cargo.toml), its [optimizer implementation](https://github.com/huggingface/candle/blob/main/candle-nn/src/optim.rs), and `footy-models` implementation. No change in this document establishes a predictive improvement or modifies production forecasts.

## A genuinely applicable problem: feasible probability distributions over starting XIs

`crates/match/src/lineup.rs::sample_lineup` builds a valid eleven-player XI by sequentially filling scarce formation slots, then remaining slots, sampling candidates using the slot-specific `selection_weight`. This is not a calibrated joint law with prescribed player-start marginals, so even perfect individual priors need not be preserved. This is independently visible in the frozen same-engine OOS: player-start Brier 0.1689 versus 0.1558 for the historical prior; the fit-only isotonic input correction does not establish W/D/L improvement.

**Necessary feasibility constraint:** if each complete XI contains exactly 11 players, the probabilities of each candidate starting must satisfy `sum_i P(i starts)=11`. This is a mathematical identity; it is not optional calibration. Recreating the `driver.rs::squad` prior formula over the historical per-fixture player CSVs, including its 18-player truncation and padding to 14, gave 112 evaluable team-fixtures with average `sum_i prior_start_i=10.325`, range 9.800–11.122. In other words, most inputs request marginals no exact-XI sampler can reproduce. The computed figures are a diagnostic derived from available 2015/16 CSVs, not a final result for the FM26 adapter. Role/availability constraints make the feasible marginal set narrower still.

**A practical proposed solution is small and exact.** First project noisy/inconsistent individual probabilities onto the feasible marginal region (record the size and reason for the projection); maintain exact 0/1 availability and confirmed-starter boundaries. Then use a maximum-entropy distribution over exactly-11 selections,

`P(S) = exp(sum_{i in S} theta_i) / Z_11(theta)`, for `|S|=11`.

If position constraints are temporarily ignored, elementary-symmetric-polynomial dynamic programming computes the normalizer in `O(n*11)` time:

`Z(i,k)=Z(i-1,k)+exp(theta_i)*Z(i-1,k-1)` with `Z(0,0)=1`.

Exact backward inclusion sampling uses probability `exp(theta_i)*Z(i-1,k-1)/Z(i,k)` and returns exactly eleven players. Fit the `theta` dual variables until the **joint law's** starter marginals reproduce feasible desired `pi_i` (`gradient log Z = pi`), with regularization, numerical stabilization and conditioning. If role/formation compatibility matters, extend the state to an 11-bit occupied-position mask and allow each player to fill at most one compatible slot; at most 2^11 masks makes explicit dynamic programming a credible research challenger for this small problem. The weighted-role model's marginals and DP cost must be tested, not assumed identical to the simple subset model.

**Relevant OpenAI math families, with exact scope:**

- [113: perfect-matching entropy and approximate counting](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/main/lean/docs/113.md). The formalized entropy bound concerns *feasible edge-marginal vectors of a perfect-matching law*, a direct mathematical analogy to player-position assignment. The entropy result does not give calibrated probabilities for footballers, but motivates constructing a distribution satisfying feasible margins rather than attempting input-side isotonic correction after arbitrary sequential sampling.
- [114: counting common matroid/polymatroid bases](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/main/CONTENTS.md). Lineup cardinality, groups of eligible players and some formation limits are set-system constraints; the paper proves approximate-counting statements for common integral bases, not a weighted, player-attributed football lineup sampler.
- [115: exact uniform contingency-table sampling](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/main/CONTENTS.md). A player-by-slot 0/1 assignment matrix has column sums 1, row sums 0 or 1, and forbidden cells for role incompatibility. The theorem covers uniform fixed-margin tables; it cannot directly sample the nonuniform, uncertain footballer selection law. As there are only eleven slots, specialized dynamic programming should precede importing any general FPRAS.
- [120: almost-linear matching](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/main/CONTENTS.md) provides fast feasibility/assignment methods, *not* a probability distribution or properly calibrated marginal probabilities.

**Tests before any promotion:** exact 11 starts; no duplicate players; confirmed boundaries honored; impossible role assignments fail closed; seed reproducibility; per-player marginal calibration against prior-available lineups; expected minutes and cross-output proper scores on the same held-out fixtures. The `sum pi=11` constraint should be logged per fixture rather than silently repaired. Because availability is also sampled, distinguish unconditional `P(start)` from `P(start | available)` and project the conditional feasible marginals within each available roster; never assume those marginals are interchangeable. Also, the historical adapter currently assigns every candidate `role_fits=1.0` for all eleven artificial role IDs, so historical role-sensitive validation requires a genuinely dated position/formation feature pipeline before it can support any claims.

## Mathematics results that do NOT transfer directly

- [139: log-concave sampling](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/main/CONTENTS.md) assumes a known minimizer and smooth potential with Hessian `I <= Hessian <= 2I`. Football's full match state is a *discrete path-dependent mixed-Poisson process*, not that target. The query-complexity theorem is not a faster simulator algorithm.
- [140: information/memory lower bounds](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/main/CONTENTS.md) concern an online learner with bounded `O(d^2)` persistent bits observing *noiseless Gaussian linear measurements*. It is not a football neural-network data-size lower bound and does not imply a transformer needs any specific number of fixtures.
- [131: switch-chain mixing for prescribed graph degree sequences](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/main/CONTENTS.md) does not improve iid full-match Monte Carlo convergence; it applies to a particular Markov chain and its stationary law.
- [374: stability of Brenier maps](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/main/CONTENTS.md) assumes a uniform source on a compact convex body and compactly supported targets. Do not infer a calibration or nonstationary football transport bound.
- [104: stochastic mean-payoff games](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/main/CONTENTS.md) computes exact values for games with *known* transition/payoff models, not event-model estimation from historical football labels.

The collection is heterogeneous and under continuing verification: the README currently states 719 manuscripts/372 families, and the [history](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/main/history.md) records manuscript withdrawals and repairs. These results should be read with their exact theorem hypotheses. No family inspected supplied a direct probabilistic football point-process model, proper-scoring calibration theorem, or empirical performance claim.

## Candle: yes for a narrowly scoped player/event model, not for the match clock

The current [Candle workspace](https://github.com/huggingface/candle/blob/main/Cargo.toml) is version `0.11.0`; [`candle-core` features](https://github.com/huggingface/candle/blob/main/candle-core/Cargo.toml) include CPU, CUDA, Metal, MKL and Accelerate, but **no native ROCm backend in `main`**. A [community ROCm implementation PR #3424](https://github.com/huggingface/candle/pull/3424) remains **open and unmerged** as of 2026-10-11 (last updated July 31), with reports of AMD consumer-GPU kernel/build workarounds; do not make it a production dependency. [`candle-nn`](https://github.com/huggingface/candle/blob/main/candle-nn/src/optim.rs) implements autodiff training and `AdamW`, so the statement that a transformer *requires* Python/CUDA inference is incorrect. Our repository pins Rust 1.75 in `rust-toolchain.toml`; verify Candle 0.11's transitive MSRV/lockfile in an isolated opt-in experiment instead of raising the production toolchain requirement blindly. For a 22-player input a CPU-only tiny MLP is likely more cost-effective than GPU setup; no measured GPU requirement exists.

**Training target:** learn strictly pre-match player-event distributions, *not* 1X2 categories independent of the simulator. A conservative first model takes dated FM ratings (fine-grained finishing, composure, passing, pace, positioning, marking, goalkeeper attributes), role, expected minutes, historical usage/form, opponent/player defense, source-missingness indicators and vintage as inputs. Predict event-rate *log residuals* and dispersion/uncertainty over the causal reference (shot occurrence/rate, creation/xA, conversion quality, discipline and minutes, each with suitable likelihood/exposure). Shrink FM effects for thin historical samples. Never use contemporary FM26 ratings to train labels in 2015/16: effective-dated FM editions are required.

**Neural candidate ladder:** (1) Bayesian/ridge or GLM regularized event-rate baseline; (2) Candle 2-layer 32–64-wide MLP on each player's features, with an **exposure-aware count loss**; (3) only after step 2 earns OOS improvement, Deep Sets (shared player MLP + masked permutation-invariant team aggregation, team-vs-opponent residual); (4) only then 1–2-layer low-width Set Transformer with role/opponent attention. A sequence Transformer with arbitrary player input order is a poor default. Compare all challengers at the **same dated training/validation/test windows** and score player event likelihoods and simulator-derived goals, exact score, 1X2 and minutes; calibration and uncertainty precede model size.

**Integration boundary:** create an optional learned-priors crate/adapter outside `crates/match` and `crates/models` hot paths, separate from the source-neutral simulation. Load audited `safetensors` once per fixture, compute the player feature matrix **once per fixture/path when role/lineup is sampled**, return versioned `PrimitivePlayerMetricForecast`/event-rate modifiers with uncertainty to existing `build_input` paths, and run `simulate_second_resolved_dynamic_mental_match` unchanged. Never execute a neural network every simulated second, nor suppress the causal channel graph. Preserve the deterministic `Rng64` for paths, seed the offline training separately, and validate exact artifact feature order, rating vintage and model/code hashes.

**Data reality:** the 11,113-row galsaar archive has historical team results and some match-level stats, but *no* historical FM ratings, lineup/player events or player identifiers. The committed 2015/16 same-engine study has only 25 held-out fixtures and uses *observed-stat-only* inputs. Neither source alone can train/test an FM-derived player transformer legitimately. Use old FM editions with dated player-level event/lineup labels, preferably multiple leagues/seasons, or defer neural claims.

**Dependency decision:** Candle should remain **experimental and optional** until a causal dataset exists, its CPU build is validated against the repository's Rust toolchain policy, and it outperforms the simpler proper-score baseline. Training with PyTorch ROCm (hardware/driver permitting) and exporting audited weights for Rust Candle inference is a fallback, not a mandatory production Python runtime.

## Important pending Dixon–Coles coherence check

`crates/match/src/calibration.rs::apply_dixon_coles` applies the classic four-cell multiplicative `tau` then renormalizes an arbitrary empirical **Monte Carlo scoreline PMF**. In the original Dixon–Coles **independent-Poisson base** the `tau` correction preserves total mass and both Poisson goal marginals. That theorem does **not** hold on an arbitrary empirical scoreline table. Explicit counterexample with lambda_home=lambda_away=0.5 and rho=-0.1:

`P(0,0)=0.4, P(1,0)=0.1, P(0,1)=0.1, P(1,1)=0.4`.

The adjusted unnormalized probabilities are `[0.410, 0.095, 0.095, 0.440]`, sum `1.04`. After renormalization, `P(home=0)` becomes `0.505/1.04=0.4855769` versus `0.5` originally. `P(away=0)` also changes. If only the exact-score table is adjusted, then the path-observed WDL, team-goal marginal/CRPS, player scorer occurrences and minute-event distributions no longer agree. Keep this variant as an explicit *alternative diagnostic forecast*, never imply it is the unchanged joint path distribution. If a low-score dependence correction qualifies on held-out proper scores, favor a **structural rate/process modification** or full-path importance weights recomputing *all* marginal outputs from the same weighted paths.

## Next falsifiable experiments (in order)

1. **Inspect feasibility/marginals of starting XI inputs** on a *different* historical period, then compare exact-size max-entropy subset/role sampler with the current greedy sampler, using start Brier, minutes MAE, and WDL/score coherence at fixed random seeds. Do not declare an entropy bound a performance improvement.
2. **Fit player event-rate GLM** with dated FM data, compare against as-of player priors; only if justified, train the identical target with a tiny Candle MLP and a masked Deep Sets model. Produce held-out log loss, CRPS, coverage and ablations by feature family. No automatic production promotion.
3. **Evaluate shared-tempo path-rate correlation vs independent Poisson and Dixon–Coles**, preserving a *single source of truth* for full-path scorelines and player-event aggregates.

Current production releases and pre-kickoff 2026 fixture artifacts remain frozen. This is a technical research specification, not backtested improvement claims.
