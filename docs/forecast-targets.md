# Version 1 football forecast targets and calibration strategy

The canonical, machine-readable contract is [`configs/forecast_targets_v1.json`](../configs/forecast_targets_v1.json). All targets must be derived from **the same set of complete, independent, pre-match second-resolved paths**, conditional on player availability and team state, unless explicitly labelled as an independent *benchmark*. Match results must never be pasted on afterward from a separate independent model; otherwise scorer and team-goal distributions stop agreeing.

## What we actually want to predict

We should *not* maximize the raw number of predicted stats or promise accuracy for every event. The unit of successful work is **a trustworthy, out-of-sample distribution that improves on a simple causal baseline**, including coverage and uncertainty. Start with four nested levels in this order:

| Priority | Layer | Preferred targets | Publishable representation | Why |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **P0** | Match | 1X2 H/D/A; joint exact score; each team's goals | One coherent path-based categorical/joint-count distribution; calibrated fair odds for 1X2 only if proven | Global sanity and historical labels widely available |
| **P0** | Selection | P(start), P(appear), minutes distribution for **registered** players | Unconditional minutes and P(start); conditional minutes given selection | Most important upstream uncertainty for player props |
| **P1** | Team | Shots, shots on target, first/second-half goals | Integer count distribution with calibrated quantiles and over/under probabilities | Drives player rates and event volume |
| **P1** | Player | P(score at least once), expected goals scored, P(assist), expected primary assists, shots/SOT, goals + assists | Unconditional over paths **and** conditional-on-playing distributions; goals and assists from the same path events | User-relevant player forecasts; avoid confusing xG with actual goals or xA with actual assists |
| **P1** | Timeline | Per-15-minute expected goals/shots, probability any goal, score state | Event counts and probabilities for disjoint clock bins; score-state history joint to each path | Useful event/momentum plots with manageable sampling noise |
| **P2** | Native one-minute events | Individual minute goal, shot and card hazards | Internally store 90-minute bins with confidence intervals; only show minute results when verified against timestamps | Per-minute probabilities are tiny, correlated, and noisy; do not interpolate from 15-minute data |
| **P2/P3** | Other events | Red cards, completed subs, yellows, corners, fouls, GK saves | Only with verified event-label feeds and event rates calibrated on *matching definitions* | Their labels/relationships differ across providers and models |
| **Display only** | Momentum | `100 × ((shots_H + 3×goals_H) − (shots_A + 3×goals_A)) / total` per window | Clearly labelled **visualization proxy**, no predictive accuracy badge | No direct observed equivalent; the `3` is arbitrary, not tuned/learned |

**Redefinition required:** "scoreline" is an empirical *joint distribution* of complete game paths, **not** a modal integer guessed from two separate expected goals. "Goal scorer" means the player responsible for a sampled actual-goal event, not the player with highest xG. "Assist" means an actual, provider-defined primary goal-assist credit, not `xA` or a fixed fraction of predicted xG. "P(player goals+assists≥1)" is calculated **within the same paths** (goals and assists are correlated), never `P(goal)+P(assist)`.

## Current capability vs target contract

The Rust engine now exports raw match H/D/A, expected team goals/shots, randomized lineups, sparse player by-minute expected shots/goals/provisional assist credits, six 15-minute plus 90 one-minute summary bins, a joint exact-score probability table, and same-path per-player P(scored at least once) / P(primary-assist credit at least once) on **new** runs. It **does not yet expose** a 1X2 post-hoc correction validated on this *exact* engine, actual player assist-identity models validated in the Premier League, observed or generated corners/yellows, or a reliable calibrated player SOT model. The older **72-path** frozen forecast has no native minute or scorer fields; never backfill those by interpolation.

Match outcome should be the *final consequence* of one underlying player+team path, but a **calibration report** may supply after-the-fact diagnostics and parameter-selection decisions based only on past data. It must never silently overwrite raw path outcomes or scoreline/player distributions with probabilities calibrated from another model family; in that case the coherent paths must be rerun or jointly reweighted with clear provenance.

The 2015–16 StatsBomb **74-match incomplete subset** provides observed player scorers, primary assists, shots and event times. In the 24 held-out covered fixtures, the historical-rate benchmark showed **no statistically clear advantage** in goal/assist timing or team shots; see [OOS audit](premier-league-player-event-oos-validation.md). That experiment **does not validate** the present FM26 Rust match simulator. A [same-engine observed-stat-only walk-forward backtest](historical-same-engine-backtest.md) of the actual Rust engine now exists on the same subset; it evaluates engine mechanics and calibration under a declared historical feature contract, never the FM26-enhanced one. Current squad mapping has no time-compatible FM editions to backtest 2015 player input using present 2026 attributes without leakage.

## Calibration design: in order, without touching sealed test matches

1. **As-of data version:** acquire historical season-by-season player memberships, match minute/event observations, injuries and lineups. Freeze source identity, publication date, team/player ID mapping, availability mask and coverage. *FM26 can only score 2026-era fixtures; historical FM should come from a historically available edition or an observed-stat-only proxy explicitly declared as a different model family.* Avoid player discovery from the future.
2. **Selection model:** calibrate P(start), P(appear) and minutes distribution from historical club lineups, then condition player-event hazards on each path's actual appearance/minutes. Evaluate unconditional and given-appearance outputs separately.
3. **Goal and shot generator:** fit the upstream player/team goal and shot rates on pre-match history, preserving red-card, fatigue, formation and lineup effects only if independently measurable. Require `shots ≥ scored non-own goals` and consistent team/player scores in each simulated path.
4. **Assists:** estimate the fraction of scored non-own goals with a provider-registered assist *only from earlier events*, then model `P(assister=j | goal, active XI, scorer, teammates)` and its uncertainty. The historical preliminary Bayesian candidate was ~0.61, but the 24 held-out 2015/16 fixtures had an 0.81 observed rate and the candidate had worse log loss than the original 0.68; **not promoted**. The simulation's default remains an explicitly uncalibrated research prior, now supplied as a typed parameter.
5. **Timing and state:** estimate goal/shot/card hazards by elapsed time and score state; test 15-minute **count**/occurrence calibration first, then 1-minute timing and score state. Assess reliability and calibration by regulation time, home/away, strong/weak teams and roster-coverage strata. No curve-fitting using the protected test period.
6. **Outcome calibration:** train any categorical/scoreline calibration on prior, genuinely OOS forecasts emitted by **this Rust engine**, then evaluate on a later untouched period. Report multiclass log loss, Brier, scoreline log score, count MAE/CRPS, event reliability plots and paired, matchday-block bootstrap intervals versus naive history-only and independent-goals baselines.
7. **Path precision:** after structural validity and event calibration, run **at least 2,500 independent full-match paths** as an *operator policy* to reduce the worst-case 1X2 sampling half-width at 95% to ~1.96 pp, and more for rare player goals/assists. These path thresholds do **not** eliminate model uncertainty. A production gate also requests at least 200 independent OOS fixtures and verified event-label coverage; those are conservative planning thresholds, not statistical sufficiency theorems.
8. **Version and promote:** freeze model code/hash, parameter fit cutoff, OOS window, input columns, seeds, label provenance, baselines, residuals and calibration. Only label a specific **target** calibrated when the same-engine data and proper-score improvement are verified. Other targets remain research-only or unavailable; no whole-model "accurate" badge.

## What actually happened in the first assist calibration experiment

See [`artifacts/validation/primary-assist-prior-2015-16-oos.json`](../artifacts/validation/primary-assist-prior-2015-16-oos.json). Strict calibration excludes fixtures on the first held-out calendar date, so 49 pre-test events matches supply 73 primary assists / 119 scored non-own goals. The smoothed posterior is about **0.610**. In the last 24 covered fixtures, there are **50 assists / 62 non-own goals** (~0.806). A model using 0.610 suffered **0.5811 log loss per goal**, compared with **0.5316** for the pre-existing 0.68 and **0.6931** for 0.50. The proposal to replace 0.68 **failed the paired loss gate**; do not silently refit it based on the 0.806 holdout proportion. The sample is incomplete and not contemporary; it says nothing definitive about the true 2026 Premier League assist propensity.

## Exact-engine OOS evidence contract

New full pipeline snapshots store `model_code_sha256`: a SHA-256 digest of the sorted Rust core/match/forecast/state domain code, Rust model-app code, Cargo manifests/lockfile and the historical-data/player/roster input scripts. This prevents accidentally certifying a changed simulation against unrelated historical scores. `check_forecast_release.py --matching-engine-oos <report.json>` requires a report carrying the **same fingerprint**, the exact engine identifier and chronological dates with a fit cutoff preceding OOS which itself ends before the new prediction cutoff. Each target must have at least 200 **distinct held-out fixtures**, a matching primary scoring rule, 95% verified label coverage, and a negative upper matchday-block bootstrap bound for *model proper-score loss minus causal-reference loss*. These are deliberately strict release-policy thresholds; no currently available same-engine evidence meets them.

A JSON report is evidence only when independently generated from the actual held-out fixture logs. The gate validates schema, cutoff ordering and numerical criteria, not the truthfulness of claims or whether the historical fixtures were genuinely untouched. Historical StatsBomb 2015/16 event-rate benchmarks are not interchangeable with the FM26 Rust player model.

## Release status and reproducible checks

Run `python3 scripts/check_forecast_release.py` to generate [`artifacts/validation/forecast-release-gates-2026-10-11.json`](../artifacts/validation/forecast-release-gates-2026-10-11.json). The saved 72-path scenario has an approx **±11.5 percentage point** *maximum 95% binomial Monte Carlo* halfwidth, before model calibration uncertainty. The release audit currently marks every quantitative target **research-only or unavailable**: there is no compatible OOS test of this FM26 engine. Momentum alone is **descriptive**. In particular, unavailable player props must **not** inherit W/D/L model confidence or historical results from a different forecasting engine.

Existing source files under `assets/predictions/2026-10-11/` were generated from the frozen research snapshot. Regenerate and publish new dated outputs **only after** a new, validated run; do not rewrite the historical predictions as if they had originally been calibrated.
